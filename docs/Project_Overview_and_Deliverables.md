# Project Overview and Deliverables

Vinyl records and CDs store their sound as microscopic variations in their grooves. When a vinyl is playing, the stylus from the record player follows the groove, and the resulting motion is transmitted through to a magnetic cartridge where relative motion between a magnet and coil changes magnetic flux, inducing voltage. If an experiment were to be conducted where light is shone on the vinyl, the resulting light intensity that is reflected depends on factors such as surface orientation, illumination direction, reflectance, roughness, and camera position. Therefore, in theory, the differing intensities of reflected light could infer the original data that the vinyl holds (music). This technique could be highly useful in the recovery of damaged vinyls, digitizing analogue records, or reading music without damaging or causing wear from the stylus and turntable.

## Deliverables

Main points of success would be measured in evidence from imaging showing that the groove features produce measurable, repeatable intensity patterns. And a final audio demonstration showing that the reconstructed groove path can produce a simulated audio that is comparable to the original.

## Workstreams

Three workstreams are needed for a 3-person group.

- Lead literature research on optical record reading and reflection physics. Select light source, camera, illumination angle, and sample geometry. Build and calibrate experiment. Primary deliverable would be a repeatable optical setup and calibrated image data.

- Model how height, slope, or groove geometry affect reflection, predict what a camera should observe from a known test pattern. Investigate whether different surface profiles produce similar reflections, comparing predictions with captured images. Main deliverable could be a physical model that explains the measured signal.

- Use inverse problems by processing and calibrating images, to produce a formulae that will generate a reconstructed groove path/profile.
